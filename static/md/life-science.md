# Life Science & Health

> Enabling progress — with transparency, consent and control.

Source: https://the-future.world/life-science/

## Progress with Trust

Life sciences are opening new possibilities — from personalised therapies to prevention, from new diagnostics to industrial biotechnology. For this progress to continue, clear rules that build trust are needed.

## Our Principles

### Health Data: Opt-in as Standard

Health data belongs to people, not to the state and not to industry. The standard is opt-in, with narrow exceptions only for public health.

### Regulation: Evidence-based and Traceable

New therapies and technologies are assessed case-by-case — based on evidence, not ideology. Regulatory decisions must be traceable and justified.

### Partnerships: Yes — but with Transparency Rules

Collaboration between government, research and industry is important. But every partnership needs clear transparency rules: who finances? Who decides? Who benefits?

## Red Lines

**No black-box medicine**: No automated decisions about medical services or treatments without traceable reasoning, human responsibility and the right to appeal.

## Why This Matters

- Pharma and biotech hubs need a clear framework to innovate responsibly
- Citizens deserve control over their health data
- Innovation and trust are not contradictory — they are prerequisites for each other

## Frequently asked questions

### Who owns health data?

The people themselves — not the state and not industry. The standard is opt-in: data is used only with explicit consent, with narrow exceptions for public health.

### Don't clear rules slow down research?

No — trust is the precondition for people sharing their data at all. Research stays strong only if innovation and trust go together: each is the condition for the other.

### What does 'no black-box medicine' mean?

No automated decisions about medical services or treatments without traceable reasoning, human responsibility and the right to appeal. That is a red line.

### Where do we stand on partnerships with industry?

Collaboration between government, research and industry is important — but every partnership needs clear transparency rules: who finances? Who decides? Who benefits?

### How are new therapies regulated?

Evidence-based and case-by-case: data instead of ideology. Regulatory decisions must be traceable and justified.
