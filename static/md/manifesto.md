# Manifesto

> Ten theses for the transition into an age of abundance: how any country or city can use artificial intelligence and robotics so that prosperity reaches everyone.

Source: https://the-future.world/manifesto/

## Zero point (0x1001)

For the first time in history, machines can do not only physical work but also cognitive work. Artificial intelligence and robotics will redistribute work, income and power, faster than most institutions can imagine. Technological change is real, but fear is optional. Yet almost everywhere, this future is discussed in the language of fear. This manifesto starts somewhere else: the future is not a fate. It is a construction project. The only open questions are who builds it, by which rules, and for whose benefit. We want prosperity instead of scarcity thinking, security instead of fear, sovereignty instead of dependency.

## Intelligence is becoming infrastructure. (0x1002)

Artificial intelligence makes cognitive work cheap, robotics makes physical work cheap. Whether artificial general intelligence arrives in five years or in twenty: we plan for the case that it comes early. Whoever has compute, models and data shapes the future. Whoever only rents them has to accept it.

## Abundance is possible, but not automatic. (0x1003)

For the first time, technology can create enough for everyone: housing, health, time. **Abundance** means prosperity through productivity, not excess through waste. Whether it reaches everyone or only a few is a political decision. Fear is no answer to that. A plan is.

## Compute is sovereignty. (0x1004)

Sovereignty in the 21st century means: your own compute, open models and enough reliable electricity to run both. Not isolation, but the ability to audit, operate and replace AI yourselves, built on open standards, transparency and security. That holds for a country, a region and a city alike.

## Production is coming back. (0x1005)

Robots in fields, on building sites and in factories make local production competitive again, including in places with high wages. Local means short distances, know-how close to home and less dependence when the world gets turbulent.

## The state becomes the best service provider around. (0x1006)

Transparent by law, open data and APIs, AI in every public office. Routine cases in minutes, every decision explained and open to appeal. Offices are filled on ability, not party membership, and politics is a phase of life, not a profession. Citizens should be able to read the state, check it and build better tools on top of it. Democracy wins when its systems are understandable, verifiable and correctable.

## No black-box decisions about people. (0x1007)

No automated decision about benefits, treatment or rights without a comprehensible justification, human responsibility and a right of appeal. Fast, but under control: powerful systems are tested before they work in critical areas.

## The dividend belongs to everyone. (0x1008)

When machines do a growing share of the work, part of the gains must flow back to everyone. A basic income as a foundation, retraining as the norm: people without existential fear retrain, start companies and try new things. Productivity creates abundance only if it is shared fairly and transitions are secured.

## Talent is the scarcest resource. (0x1009)

Natural resources matter less than minds. Every place that wants to shape the transition has to train the people who build AI and robotics, give them reasons to stay and welcome those who want to do research and found companies there, with talent visas that are fast and welcoming instead of slow and suspicious.

## Engineering instead of ideology. (0x100A)

Not left, not right, but: what works? Hypothesis, pilot, measurement, correction. We disclose what our proposals are based on and change them when the data demands it.

## Small places can go first. (0x100B)

The transition does not have to start with the largest powers. A country, a region or a city that is small enough to learn fast, able to invest and trustworthy enough to be a partner can show how it succeeds, and others can copy what works. Strong at home first, then credible abroad. One example: the Swiss chapter, Die Zukunft, wants to show it from Switzerland, for Europe and beyond.

## Our commitments (0x100C)

- **No black-box politics** No automated decisions about benefits or treatment without a comprehensible justification, human responsibility and a right of appeal.
- **Sovereign institutions, open systems** Public institutions procure critical systems only in ways that keep them auditable, portable and secure. Lock-in is a risk, and it ends.
- **Change without collapse** We rebuild welfare systems so that people can switch careers, learn and start something new without existential fear.
- **Basic income, pragmatically** We start with pilots, independently evaluated, wherever we are active, and measure the effect: bureaucracy costs, start-ups, well-being.

## What we are not (0x100D)

- **No promises of salvation.** Technology solves nothing by itself. People and institutions do.
- **No enemy images.** We argue about solutions, not about people.
- **No progress without control.** Fast, but comprehensible, verifiable and democratically steerable.
- **No programme of renunciation.** We do not administer scarcity. We end it.

November 2026. The Future. Version 1.0, 2026-11-08.
