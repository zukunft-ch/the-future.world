# Peace & Diplomacy

> Diplomacy, mediation and de-escalation as strategic strengths — strong at home first, then credible abroad.

Source: https://the-future.world/peace-neutrality/

## Peace & Diplomacy

Credibility as an honest broker is a strategic asset. Countries that mediate, keep dialogue open and de-escalate rather than inflame gain influence and security — this is not idealism, it is strategy.

## Strong at Home First, Then Credible Abroad

We want countries to work internally first: secure, resilient, sovereign. Only then can they credibly help externally. Our ambition is to lead by example — not to lecture.

## What We Stand For

- No wars of aggression and no offensive military adventures
- Diplomacy first: exhaust negotiation before escalation
- Active peace work: mediate, host talks, support humanitarian efforts
- Respect for international law and multilateral institutions

## Why a Protection and Resilience Focus Makes Sense

Threats have changed: hybrid risks, sabotage, cyber attacks, system failures. Critical infrastructure is the nervous system of modern society. Therefore: expand protection capability without becoming aggressive:

- Protect critical infrastructure (IT/OT, energy, communication, supply)
- Support civilian authorities in crises
- Clear, defensive doctrine
- High professionalism and democratic control

## Places Where Peace Is Negotiated

Every region needs trusted venues for talks, negotiations and agreements — and people trained to run them. We want to build up mediation capacity, fund it reliably and offer it consistently. In Switzerland, for example, the [Swiss chapter](https://die-zukunft.ch/en/neutrality-peace/) wants to strengthen International Geneva and the tradition of good offices.

## Guardrails

- Strictly defensive: protection, not projection
- Credible and even-handed: no tribal politics
- Transparent and rule-of-law: clear mandates, clear responsibilities

## Frequently asked questions

### Isn't diplomacy naive in a more dangerous world?

On the contrary: the more tense the world becomes, the more valuable places and people are that conflict parties can still talk to. Mediation, dialogue channels and de-escalation are hard-headed tools, not wishful thinking.

### Does peaceful mean defenceless?

No. Strictly defensive means: building protective capability without becoming aggressive — protecting critical infrastructure, supporting civilian authorities in crises, with a clear doctrine and democratic control.

### What does mediation involve in practice?

Hosting talks and negotiations, offering neutral ground and experienced mediators, keeping communication channels open in a crisis, and supporting humanitarian work.

### What does 'strong at home first, then credible abroad' mean?

A country must first work domestically: secure, resilient, sovereign. Only then can it help credibly abroad. Our ambition is to lead by example — not to lecture.

### How does this fit the new threats?

Hybrid risks, sabotage and cyberattacks target the nervous system of modern societies: their critical infrastructure. That's why peace policy and resilience belong together — protection instead of projection.

### Is this the same as Swiss neutrality?

No. Swiss neutrality is a specific, centuries-old legal status. Our international stance is broader: any country, whatever its alliances, can choose restraint, defensive doctrine and active peace work.
