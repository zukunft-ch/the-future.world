# Re-industrialisation with Robotics

> More production and value creation close to home: automated, local, open to small businesses. In the field, on the building site and in the factory.

Source: https://the-future.world/robotics/

## Why Re-industrialisation?

If we want to bring production back, it must be modern: automated, efficient, resilient. Highly automated manufacturing makes production competitive again, even where wages are high. This is an opportunity, especially for small and medium-sized enterprises.

## Robots in Every Sector

Robotics is not just a topic for the factory floor. It takes load off people wherever work is physically hard, dangerous or short of hands.

### Agriculture

Fewer people work on the land in many countries. Robotics keeps farms productive.

- Autonomous weeding, tending and harvesting robots
- Shared machinery pools so that small farms can afford robotics too
- Automated processing close to the farm

### Construction

Building faster means more housing at lower cost.

- Industrial prefabrication of components and modules
- Construction robotics for heavy, repetitive and dangerous work
- Digital building permits based on building information models

### Industry

Automation makes existing industrial strengths bigger.

- Highly automated plants for medtech, pharma, precision engineering and electronics
- Being able to make critical goods locally: medicines, components, food
- Maintenance, planning and development as new, well-paid industrial occupations

## Producing Locally

Production belongs close to the people who need it. Regional, automated plants shorten supply chains, keep know-how local and reduce dependence on global disruptions. Local does not mean isolated: trade continues in what each place does best.

## Our Priorities

- **Health & Life-Science Supply**: Medtech, pharma-adjacent production
- **Industrial Core**: Precision engineering, toolmaking, electronics/packaging
- **Resilience Basics**: Food/agri-tech, grid and supply security

## The Small-Business Package

Financing first: affordable loans, guarantees, co-investments for modernisation and (re-)shoring.

- Tax incentives for automation investments
- Shared robotics labs / automation centres
- Training and upskilling vouchers
- Faster permits for modern production facilities
- Procurement advantages for critical goods (where appropriate)
- Regulatory sandboxes for automation

## Measurable Goals

Set a public target for industrial robot density and for local industrial value creation, and report on both every year. The [Swiss chapter](https://die-zukunft.ch/en/robotics/), for example, aims to double robot density in Swiss industry by 2035.

## Automation in the Services Sector

We are pro-automation when it relieves people and increases productivity — with the clear goal: more time, more freedom, more prosperity. The gains must be shared: see [Work Without Fear](https://the-future.world/transitions/) and [Basic Income](https://the-future.world/basic-income/).

## Frequently asked questions

### Will robots take people's jobs?

Not if we do it right. Automation frees people from monotonous and dangerous work. The key lies in retraining, basic income and new job profiles.

### Do only large corporations benefit from robotics?

No — that's exactly what we want to prevent. Our small-business package includes shared robotics labs, affordable loans and training vouchers so small firms can automate too.

### What does re-industrialisation mean?

More local value creation, shorter supply chains and less dependence on global disruptions. Production close to home — modern, automated and competitive.

### Which sectors should be automated?

Wherever it makes sense: medtech, precision manufacturing, food tech, logistics, agriculture, construction. Not as an end in itself, but where automation improves quality, efficiency and resilience.

### Doesn't this require highly qualified specialists?

Yes — which is why training programmes are part of the package. Robotics expertise must not be elite knowledge. Shared labs and modular courses make entry accessible.
